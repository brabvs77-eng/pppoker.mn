Steal and Re-Steal — Taking the Blinds

Stealing blinds means making an open raise from a late position intending to win the blinds uncontested before the flop. Re-stealing means 3-betting a steal attempt to take the pot back. At Baatryn Öröö tables, the BTN and cutoff are the primary stealing positions — mastering these two moves adds consistent preflop profit to every session.

Stealing is not simply a bluff — it is a positioning play that applies fold equity where your opponents are most likely to fold. The math works: when the players remaining to act fold frequently enough, even a weak hand shows a profit as an open-raise.

Steal хэзээ?

Three conditions maximize steal EV:

  • BTN or CO — fold equity is highest late; you have the fewest players left to act and the best postflop position if called
  • Tight or passive blinds — a BB who folds to 70%+ of steal attempts makes the steal highly profitable; track this number if you can
  • 2–2.5BB sizing — a standard small open is sufficient; you do not need to commit 3BB to fold out the blinds

Open-raising for steal purposes works differently from an EP raise. From UTG, you need a strong hand because you face many players who could wake up with premiums. From the BTN, you are only threatening two players — and one of them (SB) plays from the worst position in poker.

Steal range

Position determines how wide to open:

Position Approximate open range
CO (cutoff) 25–30%
BTN (button) 40–50%
SB (vs BB only) 35–45%

These ranges look wide because they are — you are not opening for value against most of these hands. You are opening because fold equity makes it profitable. A hand like K♥ 5♥ or Q♦ 9♠ is a losing hand heads-up at showdown — but as an open-raise from BTN, it wins the pot before the flop often enough to show a profit.

Weak hands in these ranges still have postflop playability when called. You will miss most flops, but when you hit a pair, draw, or strong top pair, the positional advantage turns your marginal steal into a real value bet.

Re-steal

A re-steal is a 3-bet against an opponent you believe is stealing. It applies the same fold-equity logic back at them: they opened light, you 3-bet, and now they face a large raise with a hand they raised as a bluff.

Value re-steal range: AA, KK, QQ, AK — always 3-bet these regardless of whether you suspect a steal

Bluff re-steal range (low frequency): A5s, K9s — hands with blockers to premiums and reasonable postflop equity when called

The classic spot: BB versus BTN open. The BTN opens 45% of hands. From BB with a hand like A♠ 5♠, a 3-bet applies pressure to their wide range, blocks AA and AK in their holdings, and has backup equity if they call.

Keep re-steal bluffs at a low frequency. Most of your 3-bets from the blinds should be for value. Over-restealing turns into a chip-burning habit when opponents catch on and start 4-betting.

Fold to re-steal

When you have stolen preflop and face a 3-bet, the decision is simple:

  • 4-bet or call with premiums only — AA, KK, QQ, AK are strong enough to commit stacks
  • Fold everything else — a steal is a low-commitment play; losing it to a 3-bet is acceptable

A common leak: a player steals with K♠ T♦ from BTN, gets 3-bet, and calls "because they don't want to be pushed around." This is ego-driven decision making. When your 2.5BB open is met with a 10BB 3-bet, the pot odds and positional math rarely justify a flat call with a steal hand. Fold and move to the next steal opportunity.

Практик

Three habits that make stealing and re-stealing profitable:

  1. Steal more from BTN — every session you spend folding BTN hands is lost blind equity; opening wide from position is one of the highest-return adjustments available. See table position in poker for the full position framework.
  2. Track blind fold-to-steal stats — if the BB folds 75% to steals, open wider against them specifically; if they 3-bet frequently, tighten up and pay them off when you have premiums
  3. Re-steal for value first — most 3-bets from the blinds should be value; reserve re-steal bluffs for spots where you have strong blocker hands and a clear read that the opener is stealing

Түгээмэл асуултууд

Is a steal always a bluff?

Mostly yes in the sense that you are not opening for pure value — you are opening because fold equity makes it profitable. However, sometimes opponents call your steal, and then you need postflop basics to navigate. Being in position postflop after a steal helps enormously — you see their actions before committing more chips.

SB steal vs BB specifically?

The BB defends the widest range of any position — they already have 1BB invested and get good pot odds. Stealing into BB from SB is the hardest steal in the game because their defense range is the most elastic. From SB, raise less and 3-bet more: if you are going to play a hand against BB, a clear raise for value or fold is usually better than a limp or a marginal steal.

How does re-stealing connect to blind defense?

They are the same coin from different perspectives. When you 3-bet as re-steal, you are defending the blinds aggressively. See blind defense for the full framework — knowing when to 3-bet, call, or fold from the blinds makes both your defense and your re-steal strategy consistent and hard to exploit.
