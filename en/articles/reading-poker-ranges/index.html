Reading Poker Ranges — Fundamentals

A range is the set of hands a player could hold in a given situation. Reading ranges — narrowing that set street by street — is how strong players make better decisions than guessers. At Baatryn Öröö tables, you will never know an opponent's exact cards. You estimate probabilities and act on them.

Stop asking "does he have ace-king?" Start asking "what percentage of his range beats me, and does my bet make those hands fold?" Range thinking connects every street from preflop opens to river bluffs.

What is a range?

Example: button open ≈ 40% of hands — AA, 72o, suited connectors, and everything between depending on the player. You are not assigning one hand; you are assigning a group.

One hand is a point. A range is a cloud of possible hands weighted by how likely each is given the action.

Foundation: preflop strategy teaches opening ranges by position. Start there before postflop narrowing.

Step 1: Preflop action

Action Typical range
UTG raise Tight (~10%)
BTN raise Wide (~40%)
3-bet Premiums + some bluffs
Call Medium strength — pairs, suited hands, broadways

A UTG raise and a BTN raise represent completely different ranges even though both are "a raise." Position and sizing tell you how wide or tight they are.

Add player tendencies: a nit's BTN open might be 25%; a lag's might be 55%. Use poker statistics analysis — VPIP, PFR, 3-bet% — to calibrate.

Step 2: Flop action

Postflop action narrows the preflop range:

  • C-bet → favors hands that connect with the board plus bluffs from the aggressor
  • Check → can mean weakness, pot control, or a trap
  • Raise → usually strong — two pair+, sets, strong draws, occasional bluffs

Board texture matters. On K♠ 7♦ 2♣, a c-bettor has many kings, overpairs, and air. On J♠ T♦ 9♠, their range includes more connectors and flush draws that hit.

Pair flop reads with c-bet strategy and postflop basics.

Step 3: Narrow street by street

Each street removes hands that would have played differently.

Example line: BTN open → flop c-bet → turn check → river bet

Interpretation: flop c-bet includes value and bluffs. Turn check removes many strong hands (they would bet again) and some bluffs (they would barrel). River bet after checking turn is usually value — polarized to strong hands that slow-played or woke up on the river.

This is not certainty. It is weighted logic. "Usually value" still means bluffs exist — just fewer of them.

Blockers

Your hole cards remove combinations from opponent ranges:

  • A♠ in your hand — opponent cannot have the nut flush if spades are relevant
  • K♠ Q♠ in your hand — fewer combos of AK, KQ, QQ in their value range

Blockers matter most in bluff spots. Holding the A♣ on a three-club board makes nut flush bluffs more credible because they cannot have the ace of clubs.

Read the art of bluffing for blocker-based bluff selection.

Multiway range reading

With three or more players, ranges are tighter on average. Someone almost always has a piece of the board. Adjust — see multiway pot strategy.

A c-bet in a 4-way pot represents a stronger range than a c-bet heads-up because the bettor expects multiple callers.

Exploiting range weaknesses

Once you estimate a range, find leaks:

  • They fold too much to 3-bets → 3-bet bluff wider (3-bet and 4-bet strategy)
  • They call too much → value bet thinner, bluff less
  • They c-bet too much → check-raise more (check-raise tactics)

Advanced exploit concepts: exploitation tactics (Mongolian article).

Turn and river ranges

Ranges are narrowest on the river. There are no more cards coming — only value bets, bluffs, and showdown.

Connect range reading to turn and river play. River decisions depend entirely on what hands remain in their range after every prior action.

Practical workflow

  1. Preflop action → assign initial range by position and stats
  2. Each postflop action → remove hands inconsistent with that play
  3. Compare your hand vs their remaining range — are you ahead often enough?
  4. Adjust for opponent type — stations keep more weak hands; nits fold most bluffs
  5. Review hands after sessions — where did you misread the range?

Common mistakes

  • Assigning one specific hand and committing to it
  • Ignoring position when building ranges
  • Not updating the range after each street
  • Assuming checks always mean weakness
  • Forgetting your blockers and theirs

FAQ

Can I know their exact hand?

No. Poker is probabilities. "He probably has a strong king or an overpair" is the right level of precision.

How does range reading connect to pot odds?

Once you estimate how often you are ahead, compare that to the price you are getting. Pot odds and equity turns range estimates into math.

What about floating?

Calling a c-bet in position with intent to take the pot later is called floating. See floating strategy for a related tactic.
