Bet Sizing — When and How Many BB

Bet sizing is not just how much you put in the pot. It sends information, extracts value, and makes bluffs work. Too small and you miss value. Too large and only the nuts call. At Baatryn Öröö cash tables, sizing separates winning players from break-even ones.

Consistent sizing is harder to exploit. Varying bet size only with hand strength is a leak good opponents will punish.

Why it matters

  • Your bet narrows what opponents continue with
  • Pot odds change for everyone still in the hand
  • Bluff success depends on giving bad odds to call
  • Value bets need sizes that weaker hands can call

Standard sizes

Size % of pot When
25–33% Small Dry boards, thin value, bluffs
50–66% Medium Standard value, c-bets
75–100% Large Wet boards, strong hands, protection
Overbet 100%+ Nut hands, polarized ranges

Preflop sizing

  • Open: 2.5–3 BB (+ 1 BB per caller)
  • 3-bet: roughly 3× the open (in position) or 3.5× (out of position)
  • 4-bet: 20–25% of stack or all-in when short

More detail: preflop strategy.

Flop c-bet (continuation bet)

If you were the preflop aggressor, you can bet first on the flop.

  • Dry board (K72 rainbow): 25–33% pot is enough — few draws
  • Wet board (JT9 two-tone): 66–75% or check — many draws need charging
  • Multiway (3+ players): c-bet less, check more — someone likely connected

Board texture matters more than your exact hand sometimes.

Value betting

When you have a strong hand, bet to get more money in.

  • Weaker hands may call → medium size (50–66%)
  • Nut hand with draws on board → large bet (75–100%)
  • Thin value on second pair → small bet (25–33%)

Ask: "What worse hands call this size?"

Bluff betting

You need fold equity. Size should make opponents' pot odds bad.

  • Small bluff: 33% pot on scary runouts
  • Large bluff (polarized): 75%+ on river when ranges are capped
  • Semi-bluff with draws: medium size builds pot when you improve

Art of bluffing — timing and reads matter as much as size.

Turn and river

  • Turn: pot is bigger — you can size up from flop
  • River: value or bluff — pick one. Medium "blocking" bluffs often get called by everything
  • Overbet river when you block nut hands or opponent's range is capped

Stack depth at 250/500

10,000₮ stack = 20 BB. You cannot bet 50% pot three streets without going all-in. Plan streets ahead. See cash games Mongolia.

Practical rules

  1. Default c-bet: 50% pot on favorable boards
  2. For value, think in calls — will worse hands call?
  3. Beginners: avoid betting more than 20% of stack on one street
  4. Pair sizing with postflop basics framework
  5. Track results — same size with nuts and air is readable

FAQ

Is pot-sized bet always correct?

No. Good on nut hands and wet boards. Often too much on dry boards where small bets get the same calls.

Should I min-bet?

Usually bad — you give great pot odds and miss value. Rare exploit spots only.

How does sizing relate to position?

In position you can bet smaller and still get folds. Out of position often needs larger bets for protection.

Bet sizing at VIP tables?

Higher stakes players notice sizing tells faster. Study VIP tables before moving up.

Sizing tells to avoid

Betting tiny with monsters and huge with bluffs is readable. Use similar sizes with value and bluffs on the same board texture.

On the river, small bets get called by any pair. Large bets fold weak pairs but get called by better. Match size to goal.

Stack commitment

If a bet commits 30%+ of your stack, ask if you would call a shove. At 20 BB in cash games, one pot bet often means all-in.

Review 10 showdowns per session. Adjust sizing next time.

Consistent sizing across similar spots makes you harder to read. Varying bet size only with hand strength is an exploit good opponents will find within one session.
