Omaha (PLO) Rules — Complete Guide

Omaha (PLO) is poker with 4 hole cards instead of 2. To make a hand you must use exactly 2 hole cards + 3 community cards. More draws, bigger pots, more action. Baatryn Öröö runs 6-max Omaha tables 24/7.

Omaha is not "Hold'em with extra cards." The 2+3 rule changes everything. Master Hold'em rules first, then add Omaha when your bankroll and skills are ready.

Main differences from Hold'em

Hold'em Omaha (PLO)
Hole cards 2 4

Full comparison: Hold'em vs Omaha.

The 2+3 rule — most important

In Omaha you use only 2 of your 4 hole cards and exactly 3 from the board. No exceptions.

Example: You hold A♠ K♠ Q♦ J♦. Board: A♥ K♥ Q♠ 10♣ 2♦.

  • Wrong: A♠ K♠ Q♠ 10♣ 2♦ (3 hole + 2 board)
  • Correct: A♠ K♠ + A♥ K♥ Q♠ = Two Pair, Aces and Kings

Beginners often think they have a straight or flush when they only used one hole card. The dealer awards the pot to the correct hand at showdown.

Game flow

Preflop → flop → turn → river — same stages as Hold'em. Betting actions (fold, check, call, raise, all-in) work identically.

Omaha pots grow faster because more players connect with the board. Position and pot odds matter even more than in Hold'em.

Hand rankings

Poker hand rankings use the same order as Hold'em. In Omaha, Full House, Flush, and Straight appear more often because 4 hole cards create more combinations.

A "wrap" straight draw in Omaha can have 13+ outs. That is why pots get large and swings feel bigger.

Starting hands (brief)

Premium Omaha starting hands include:

  • AAxx double-suited (aces with two suits covered)
  • KK, QQ with suited connectors
  • A♠K♠Q♠J♠ (maximum wrap potential)
  • Hands that flop nut draws

Avoid four low disconnected cards. They look playable but rarely make the nuts.

Bankroll

Omaha has higher variance than Hold'em. Bankroll management: Hold'em needs 30–40 buy-ins; Omaha 50–70 buy-ins is recommended.

At 250/500 Omaha, a 25,000₮ stack is only 50 big blinds. That is short for PLO. Buy in deeper if the table allows.

Getting started

  1. Build a solid base in Hold'em cash games
  2. Choose a 250/500 Omaha table
  3. Play only strong starting hands with good connectivity
  4. Practice pot control — do not put weak hands into huge pots
  5. Study bet sizing for multiway pots

Common mistakes

  • Misreading hands (using 3 or 4 hole cards)
  • Overvaluing two pair on wet boards
  • Playing Omaha with a Hold'em-sized bankroll
  • Ignoring nut vs second-nut draws
  • Chasing non-nut flushes

FAQ

Why 4 hole cards?

More combinations, more action. Pros often call PLO an "action game." Bigger pots attract experienced players.

What is a nut flush?

The highest possible flush on the board. In Omaha, second-nut draws lose often. Always ask: "Can someone have better?"
