C-Bet Strategy — Continuation Bet

C-bet (continuation bet) is when the preflop aggressor bets first on the flop. It is one of the most common postflop plays in Hold'em. At Baatryn Öröö tables, Mongolian regulars use c-bets every session — done right, they win more pots and force more folds.

If you raised preflop, the flop is your chance to keep the story going. A well-sized c-bet says "I still have a strong hand." A well-timed check says "I need to slow down." Knowing which is which separates winning players from leaking ones.

What is a c-bet?

You raised preflop → you bet first on the flop. That is a continuation bet. You are continuing the aggression you showed before the board came.

The logic is simple: as the raiser, your range includes more strong hands than a caller's range. On many flops, you have range advantage — more top pairs, overpairs, and strong draws. Betting capitalizes on that edge.

When to c-bet

Condition C-bet?
Dry board (K♠ 7♦ 2♣ rainbow) Yes — 25–33% pot
Your range connects better Yes
You have the best hand Value + protection
Wet board (J♠ T♦ 9♠ two-tone) Careful — 50–66% or check
Multiway 3+ players Reduce frequency

Dry boards miss most calling ranges. A single bet often wins the pot. Wet boards connect with suited connectors, pairs, and straight draws — your opponent may have hit hard, so bet bigger for value or check and reassess.

Think about what your opponent called preflop. A tight UTG caller has a stronger range than a loose button caller. Adjust c-bet frequency accordingly.

Bet sizing

  • Standard: 50% of the pot
  • Dry boards: 25–33% is enough — small bet, high fold equity
  • Wet boards: 66–75% — deny good pot odds to draws

Smaller bets on dry flops risk less when you are bluffing and still get folds. Larger bets on wet flops charge draws and build pots when you have top pair or better.

When to check (give up)

Not every flop deserves a c-bet. Check when:

  • You have pure air with no equity — do not bluff every street
  • The board is wet, your hand is weak, and you are out of position (OOP)
  • Your opponent rarely folds to c-bets — exploit later with value bets instead

Checking is not weak. It saves chips and keeps your checking range balanced. When you check strong hands sometimes (traps) and weak hands other times, opponents cannot auto-fold or auto-call against you.

In position vs out of position

In position (IP): c-bet more often. You act last on every street, so you see what your opponent does before you commit more chips. IP c-bets are profitable on a wider range of boards.

Out of position (OOP): check more. Check-call and check-raise tactics become your main weapons. Betting every flop OOP bloats pots when you are behind and cannot control the action.

C-bet frequency in practice

A balanced c-bet frequency against a single opponent is roughly 55–65% on average — not 100%, not 30%. Mix value bets, semi-bluffs, and pure bluffs so opponents cannot exploit you.

Steps for each flop:

  1. Do I have the best hand or strong equity? → c-bet for value or semi-bluff
  2. Does this board favor my range or theirs? → bet if you have range advantage
  3. How many opponents? → fewer c-bets in multiway pots
  4. What do stats say? → check poker statistics analysis for fold-to-c-bet

After they call your c-bet

When an opponent calls your flop c-bet, their range is stronger than if they had folded. They likely have a pair, a draw, or a slow-played monster.

On the turn, revisit the same questions: am I still ahead? Does this card help their range? Should I barrel again or check for pot control?

Turn and river decisions follow a clear framework: turn and river play.

Common mistakes

  • C-betting 100% of flops — predictable and exploitable
  • Same bet size on every board — dry and wet flops need different sizing
  • C-betting multiway with air — someone almost always has something
  • Ignoring position — OOP c-bets need a stronger reason
  • Giving up too late — if you c-bet flop and turn with no equity, do not fire river bluffs without a plan

Practical checklist

  1. On the flop: do I have the best hand or good equity? → c-bet
  2. One opponent heads-up → aim for 55–65% c-bet frequency
  3. Track fold-to-c-bet in your HUD or notes
  4. Pair c-bet strategy with pot odds and equity when facing raises
  5. Review sessions — note spots where checking would have saved money

FAQ

Should I c-bet every flop?

No. Around 60–70% is a solid target heads-up. Sometimes check is the highest-EV play — especially OOP on wet boards.

What if they raise my c-bet?

Fold weak hands and bluffs. Call or re-raise with strong value and nut draws. Their raise narrows their range — use reading poker ranges to decide.

How does c-bet connect to preflop?

You can only c-bet if you were the preflop aggressor. If you called preflop, you are the caller — different strategy applies. Start with solid preflop strategy and Hold'em rules if you are new.

C-bet called — what about the turn?

Use the turn-river framework. Barrel when you have equity or range advantage; check when marginal or beat.
