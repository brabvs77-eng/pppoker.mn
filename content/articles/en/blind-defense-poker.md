# Blind Play — SB and BB

The **blinds** are forced bets — you are already in the pot before seeing your cards. Small Blind (SB) and Big Blind (BB) are the worst positions in poker because you act last preflop and first on every postflop street. At [Baatryn Öröö](https://pppoker.mn/?lang=en) tables, Mongolian regulars consistently lose the most from the blinds — but with the right approach, you can cut those losses and even turn a profit.

The core challenge is simple: every hand played from the blinds is an uphill fight. You pay to play and then face a positional disadvantage that follows you all the way to the river. Getting this spot right separates break-even players from winning ones.

## Яагаад хэцүү вэ?

Playing from the blinds is harder than any other position for three compounding reasons:

- **Postflop OOP** — you act first on the flop, turn, and river, giving opponents the informational advantage on every street
- **EP open disadvantage** — when an early-position player raises, their range is strong; you are the last to know and the first to act postflop
- **Constant pressure** — you will fold more hands than from any other position, meaning your blind investment is lost frequently

This does not mean you should fold every hand from the blinds. It means you must be selective and understand your options.

## BB defense

When facing an open raise, the Big Blind has three choices: call, 3-bet, or fold. The pot odds from the BB are better than from any other position — you already have 1BB invested, so the price to call is discounted.

| Hand | Action |
|------|--------|
| Premium (AA, KK, QQ, AK) | 3-bet |
| Playable (suited connectors, Axs, medium pairs) | Call — pot odds are good |
| Trash (offsuit low cards, no connectivity) | Fold |

Because 1BB is already in the pot, [pot odds](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/pot-odds-equity/) favor calling a wider range than you might expect. Against a BTN raise to 2.5BB, you are getting roughly 3:1 — enough to call many speculative hands that would be folds from other positions.

Against tight early-position openers, tighten your defense range. Against loose button and cutoff steals, widen it. Your defense frequency should respond to your opponent's opening range, not follow a fixed rule.

## SB тоглоом

The Small Blind is the worst seat in poker. You act last preflop but first postflop, combining the worst of both worlds. From the SB:

- **Fold often** — the SB loses more per hand than the BB on average; accepting folds is correct
- **3-bet or fold** — calling from SB is rarely the best line; it creates a bloated pot where you must act first postflop
- **[Steal-resteal](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/steal-resteal-poker/)** — when the BTN opens, SB 3-bets are your primary weapon; flat-calling sets up difficult postflop spots

A common mistake is limping or flat-calling from SB to "see a cheap flop." Against an aggressive table, that cheap flop quickly becomes an expensive one when you have no position and a marginal hand.

## 3-bet блайндаас

Three-betting from the blinds is your main aggressive weapon OOP. Structure your 3-bet range with two components:

**Value:** AA, KK, QQ, AK, AQ — hands that want maximum money in preflop

**Bluff (low frequency):** A5s, KQs — hands with blockers or good equity when called

When you 3-bet from the BB, the open raiser is getting less attractive pot odds than if you called. That forces more folds and builds pots when you have the best hand. Sizing from the blinds: 3.5–4× the open raise is standard, slightly larger than in position to compensate for your postflop disadvantage.

Do not over-bluff from the blinds — your range is already committed to playing OOP, so mix in enough value to make your 3-bets credible.

## Postflop OOP

Once you have called preflop and are playing postflop out of position, your playbook has three main lines:

- **Check-call** with medium-strength hands — keep pot small, realize equity, avoid building pots you may lose
- **Check-raise** with strong hands and strong draws — see [check-raise tactics](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/check-raise-tactics/) for full detail
- **Check-fold** with weak hands and no equity — do not bluff-call off chips in poor spots

The check-raise is your most important OOP weapon. When the preflop aggressor c-bets and you have a set, two pair, or a strong flush draw, check-raising forces them to call a large bet at a disadvantage. It builds pots when you are ahead and adds fold equity when you are behind with draws.

Do not lead-bet (donk bet) OOP without a clear reason. Most of the time, checking and reacting to the aggressor's c-bet is more effective and harder to exploit.

## Практик

Three principles cover most blind situations:

1. **BB defense: target 25–35% call frequency at a 6-max table** — wide enough to be unexploitable, tight enough to avoid playing too many junk hands OOP
2. **SB: fold more, 3-bet more** — calling from SB sets up the worst postflop scenarios; prefer a clear raise or a clean fold
3. **Against steal attempts — do not overfold** — defend some weaker hands based on [pot odds and equity](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/pot-odds-equity/); players who fold too much from the blinds get exploited by aggressive stealers

## Түгээмэл асуултууд

### Should I defend every hand from BB?

No. Pot odds make the BB defense wider than most positions, but trash hands still fold. Factor in pot odds, opponent range, and your postflop skills. Start at roughly 25–35% defense in 6-max.

### Blinds at 250/500 — should I play differently?

Micro stakes players often fold too much from the blinds. Keep [bankroll management](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/bankroll-management/) at 30+ buy-ins for micro stakes and do not be afraid to defend with speculative hands when pot odds are correct.

### What about against short stacks?

Short-stack opponents have less fold equity, and all-in ranges change drastically. Against a short stack's shove, use a push-fold chart rather than postflop strategy. Against short stacks open-raising, tighten your 3-bet bluffs and lean more toward value.
