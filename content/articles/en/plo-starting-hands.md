# PLO Starting Hands — Opening Range

**PLO starting hand selection is completely different from Hold'em.** You receive four cards, but you must use exactly two from your hand and three from the board — the famous [Omaha rule](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/omaha-rules-complete/). This makes hand connectivity, suitedness, and coordination far more important than raw card strength. At [Baatryn Öröö](https://pppoker.mn/?lang=en) 6-max Omaha tables, this range discipline is the foundation everything else builds on.

The most common beginner mistake in PLO: overvaluing a hand because it has a high pair or one strong combination. In PLO, all four cards must work together. A hand with one strong combination and three dead cards is a losing proposition in the long run.

## Суурь дүрэм

Three rules govern PLO hand selection:

- **The 2+3 rule** — you must use exactly two hole cards and three board cards. Remember [Omaha rules](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/omaha-rules-complete/). A hand that looks like a flush with four suited cards in hand only qualifies as a flush if two of them plus three board cards make the hand.
- **All four cards must work together** — look for four cards that connect, suit, or reinforce each other; "dead" cards reduce your postflop potential dramatically
- **Ragged four cards = fold** — four cards with no connectivity, no suitedness, and no meaningful combinations are a clear fold regardless of one high card

These rules filter out the hands that beginners play and lose with. Apply them before looking at position or read.

## Premium гар

The best PLO hands combine high card strength, double-suitedness, and connectivity. Here are the categories from strongest to solid:

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| AAxx double-suited | A♠ A♥ K♠ Q♥ |
| KKxx double-suited | K♠ K♥ J♠ T♥ |
| Double-suited rundown | Q♠ J♠ T♥ 9♥ |
| A♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ — single-suited Broadway | Nut potential |

**Double-suited** means two pairs of suits among the four cards, giving two independent flush draws on the right boards. This adds significant equity versus a single-suited hand.

**Rundowns** are consecutive or near-consecutive cards (like T♥ 9♥ 8♠ 7♠). They generate powerful wrap straight draws on coordinated flops and are among the most profitable hands in PLO even without high pairs.

**AAxx** is the strongest preflop holding in PLO, but it is far weaker than AA in Hold'em. On a coordinated board with a flush draw and straight draws in play, bare aces without additional nut draw potential can lose substantial pots.

## Тоглохгүй

Some hands look attractive but are consistently losing propositions in PLO:

- **Four disconnected cards** — J♠ 8♦ 4♣ 2♥ has no flush potential, no straight potential, and generates nothing useful on almost any board
- **Rainbow raggy hands** — four different suits with no connectivity means no wrap potential, no flush draws, no combinations
- **Weak OOP hands** — marginal speculative hands (medium pairs with no connectivity) are borderline profitable in position but clear folds OOP

The fundamental error is treating PLO like Hold'em and playing any hand with an ace or king. High cards without connectivity or suitedness are traps. An ace in PLO only matters when it anchors a nut flush draw or when it pairs on a board where you can use it.

## Позиц

Position matters in PLO for the same reason it does in Hold'em — acting last postflop gives you information before committing chips. Adjust your opening range by position:

- **EP (early position):** Premium hands only — AAxx double-suited, KKxx double-suited, top rundowns. The OOP postflop disadvantage means you need the strongest hands to overcome it.
- **BTN (button):** Widen to include more rundowns, suited connectors, and double-suited speculative hands. Position compensates for slightly weaker hand strength.
- For the full position framework, see [table position in poker](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/table-position-poker/) — the same principles apply in PLO.

In 6-max PLO, there is more action than in full-ring, so the BTN and cutoff ranges open wider. Being on the button with even a medium rundown is often profitable against predictable opponents.

## Банкролл

PLO has significantly higher variance than Hold'em. When big draws clash with made hands, enormous pots are won and lost on equity flips. You will experience larger swings even when playing correctly.

The standard recommendation for PLO bankroll: **50–70 buy-ins** for your target stake. See [bankroll management](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/bankroll-management/) for full guidance. Underbankrolled PLO players get forced off tables by variance before their edge materializes over a sufficient sample.

## Практик

Three priorities for PLO starting hand play:

1. **Pursue double-suited hands** — the flush draw potential they add is consistently valuable; prefer double-suited over single-suited versions of otherwise equivalent hands
2. **Play tight from EP** — the positional disadvantage postflop costs enough that only premium holdings justify the preflop investment
3. **Draw potential = hand value** — connect strong starting hands to their postflop draw potential with [PLO draw strategy](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/plo-draw-rules/)

## Түгээмэл асуултууд

### Is AA always a strong hand in PLO?

Usually yes preflop, but with important caveats. Bare aces (AA with two disconnected low cards) are vulnerable on any coordinated board. In multiway pots with a low board full of possible draws, AAxx can be in trouble against a range of wrap draws and set-draw combinations. Double-suited AAxx with connected side cards is the premium version. Always check what the board does to your non-ace cards.

### Should I start with Hold'em before PLO?

Highly recommended. [Hold'em vs Omaha](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/holdem-vs-omaha/) covers the key differences. Hold'em teaches position, pot odds, and postflop reasoning in a simpler format. PLO adds four-card complexity on top of those foundations. Jumping into PLO without Hold'em fundamentals means learning two games at once.

### How does PLO 6-max change starting hand requirements?

In 6-max, fewer players at the table means blinds come around faster and stealing becomes more relevant. BTN and CO opening ranges widen relative to full-ring. Rundowns and double-suited hands gain value because of the more aggressive, positional game. The fundamentals of connectivity and suitedness remain the same — the ranges simply adjust wider.
