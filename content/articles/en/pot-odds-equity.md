# Pot Odds, Equity, and Outs — Math Basics

**Pot odds** compare what is in the pot to what you must call. **Equity** is your chance to win the hand. Know both and your call/fold decisions improve immediately. At [Baatryn Öröö](https://pppoker.mn/?lang=en) cash tables, this math appears every session.

You do not need a calculator. Basic outs and the rule of 2 and 4 cover most spots.

## What are pot odds?

**Formula:** Pot odds = Amount to call / (Pot + Amount to call)

**Example:** Pot is 1000₮, opponent bets 500₮. You must call 500₮.
- Total pot after your call: 1000 + 500 + 500 = 2000₮
- Pot odds: 500 / 2000 = **25%**

You need more than 25% equity to call profitably (ignoring implied odds).

## What is equity?

**Equity** = probability of winning with your current hand, expressed as a percentage.

Example: flush draw with 9 outs and 2 cards to come. Roughly 35% equity to hit by the river.

Equity changes every street as cards are revealed and ranges narrow.

## Outs — how many cards help?

An **out** is a card that improves your hand to a likely winner.

| Draw | Outs (approx.) |
|------|----------------|
| Flush draw (9 remaining) | 9 |
| Open-ended straight | 8 |
| Gutshot straight | 4 |
| Two overcards | 6 |
| Set vs overpair | 2 |

**Rule of 2 and 4:**
- On the flop: outs × 4 ≈ % to improve by river
- On the turn: outs × 2 ≈ % on river only

9 outs on the flop: 9 × 4 = 36% equity approximately.

## Call or fold — step by step

1. Count outs or estimate equity
2. Calculate pot odds for the call
3. Equity > pot odds → **call** (or raise as semi-bluff)
4. Equity < pot odds → **fold**

**Example:** 9 outs ≈ 36% equity. Opponent bets half pot (you need 25%). Call is correct.

**Counter-example:** Gutshot with 4 outs ≈ 16% on the turn. Opponent bets full pot (you need 33%). Fold.

## Implied odds

Chance to win extra money on future streets when you hit. Example: nut flush draw against a strong hand that may stack off.

Beginners should not overuse implied odds. Use them on clear nut draws, not gutshots.

## Reverse implied odds

Risk of hitting your draw but still losing (second nut flush, bottom two pair on scary boards). Factor this in before calling large bets.

## Practice at the table

1. Memorize 9 outs for a flush draw, 8 for an open-ender
2. Compare bet size to pot — half pot ≈ 25% odds, full pot ≈ 33%
3. Large bets worsen pot odds — fold marginal draws ([bet sizing](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/bet-sizing-poker/))
4. Protect [bankroll](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/bankroll-management/) — avoid hope calls

## Common mistakes

- Calling because a draw "looks good" without counting
- Calling big bets with gutshots (4 outs)
- Ignoring pot odds on all-in calls
- Chasing on the river with no odds
- Forgetting opponent can have better when you hit
