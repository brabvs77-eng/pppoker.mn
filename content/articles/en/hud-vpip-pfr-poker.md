# HUD: VPIP, PFR, AF — Deep Dive

**HUD** (Heads-Up Display) shows opponent statistics overlaid on the poker table. On PPPoker, full HUD functionality is limited, but understanding these three core numbers — VPIP, PFR, and Aggression Factor — is essential for every serious player. At [Baatryn Öröö](https://pppoker.mn/?lang=en) tables, even manual tracking of these stats transforms how you read opponents. See the broader framework in [poker statistics analysis](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/poker-statistics-analysis/).

Statistics replace guesswork. Instead of estimating what a player might do, you have observed data. Three numbers — VPIP, PFR, AF — paint a picture of how loose or tight, aggressive or passive, any opponent is.

## VPIP (Voluntarily Put $ In Pot)

VPIP measures what percentage of hands a player voluntarily invests money preflop — excluding forced blind posts. It is your primary "how loose is this player?" metric.

| VPIP | Player type |
|------|-------------|
| 15–22% | Tight |
| 22–30% | Regular |
| 30%+ | Loose |

A player with VPIP 45% is playing nearly half of all hands — many of those will be speculative or marginal. Against them, thin value bets become more profitable because they reach showdown with weaker holdings. A player at VPIP 15% plays only premium hands — steal their blinds freely and respect their bets.

VPIP alone does not tell you how aggressive someone is; it only tells you how wide they play preflop. Pair it with PFR to see the full picture.

## PFR (Pre-Flop Raise)

PFR measures what percentage of hands a player raises preflop. Compare it directly to VPIP to understand their preflop style.

| PFR | What it means |
|-----|---------------|
| Close to VPIP | Aggressive — rarely limps, raises most hands played |
| Much lower than VPIP | Passive — limps frequently, calls more than raises |
| PFR 20%+ | Tight-aggressive (TAG) — the most dangerous player type |

**The gap between VPIP and PFR matters.** A player with VPIP 35% and PFR 8% limps heavily — they are passive and loose. Value bet wide and do not respect their raises. A player with VPIP 24% and PFR 22% is nearly all-in preflop every time they play — they have strong hands and a clear plan.

Against high limpers (large VPIP-PFR gap), iso-raise to isolate them heads-up with your strong hands. See [exploitation tactics](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/exploitation-tactics/) for the full playbook.

## AF (Aggression Factor)

AF is calculated as **(Bet + Raise) ÷ Call**. It measures postflop aggression — how often a player bets and raises versus calls.

- **AF 2+** — aggressive postflop; they barrel, bet thin, and rarely give up
- **AF 1–2** — balanced; a mix of bets and calls
- **AF below 1** — passive; they prefer calling to betting

A high AF does not always mean they have a strong hand — it means they apply pressure. Against high-AF players, widen your calling range with decent made hands because they barrel with bluffs and draws. Against low-AF players, be suspicious when they finally do bet — it often means real strength.

AF combined with VPIP and PFR gives you a three-dimensional read: wide + passive + low AF = calling station. Tight + aggressive + high AF = solid TAG.

## Хэрхэн ашиглах вэ?

Combine all three stats to identify the player type, then apply the right counter-strategy:

1. **Loose-passive** (VPIP 40+, PFR 10, AF 0.8) — value bet large, bluff zero, let them call down with second pair
2. **Tight** (VPIP 15, PFR 12) — steal their blinds frequently; when they 3-bet, fold everything except premiums
3. **Wait for 50+ hand sample** — below 30–40 hands, stats are unreliable noise; do not over-adjust on a short sample

Once you have the profile, the next step is [exploitation tactics](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/exploitation-tactics/) — matching the stat read to a specific counter-strategy.

## PPPoker дээр

PPPoker does not support a full real-time HUD. Your alternatives:

- **Hand notes during session** — note fold-to-c-bet, 3-bet frequency, and showdown tendencies after each relevant hand
- **Post-session analysis** — review hand histories and manually log key stat observations per player
- **Observe fold-to-c-bet and 3-bet %** — the two most predictive stats for immediate table adjustments

Even without a HUD, a session journal with VPIP estimates and fold-to-c-bet notes gives you an edge over players who rely on pure guesswork.

## Практик

Three steps to put stats to work at the table:

1. **Know your own VPIP/PFR** — before tracking others, understand what your own numbers look like; most players are looser and more passive than they think
2. **Decide after 20+ opponent hands** — do not 3-bet bluff a player on hand 3 because they folded once; get a sample first
3. **Combine stats with [bankroll management](https://pppoker.mn/en/articles/bankroll-management/)** — moving up stakes means facing better players with fewer obvious leaks; stats-based adjustments matter more as the field tightens

## Түгээмэл асуултууд

### What VPIP should I aim for?

Beginners: 22–28% is a reasonable target — not so tight you miss value, not so loose you bleed chips. Regulars in 6-max: 24–30%. The right number also depends on table composition — if your table is full of nits, opening wider is profitable.

### Why is PFR usually lower than VPIP?

Because some percentage of hands are played as limps or flat calls rather than raises. A large gap (e.g. VPIP 40%, PFR 12%) means the player limps and calls frequently — a passive style. This is almost always exploitable: iso-raise their limps and value bet their calling range relentlessly.

### Can I track stats manually in PPPoker?

Yes. Use the note feature on the player profile or keep a simple session log. After each session, record notable opponents' estimated VPIP, how often they folded to c-bets, and whether they showed down weak hands. Over multiple sessions, patterns emerge even without automated tracking.
